# Focused novelty screen — 18 September 2026

This is a rapid screen, not a systematic review or an established novelty claim.
Searches combined stroke survivors, NHIS, cost-related medication nonadherence,
affordability, healthcare access, and contemporary 2019–2025 periods.

1. **Food Insecurity and Perceived Financial Stress are Associated with
   Cost-related Medication Non-adherence in Stroke** (2023; PMID 37464522):
   NHIS 2014–2018 stroke survivors, with age-specific financial stress and
   medication-affordability associations.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10361605/
2. **Changes in cost-related nonadherence among US adults with multiple chronic
   conditions from 2019 to 2023** (2025): contemporary NHIS repeated cross-section,
   working-age adults with multiple conditions, including stroke among the
   qualifying conditions. This overlaps with the medication and pandemic-trend
   framing and must be discussed in a manuscript.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12204332/
3. **Younger Stroke Survivors Have Reduced Access to Physician Care and
   Medications: National Health Interview Survey From Years 1998 to 2002**:
   prior evidence that younger survivors face access barriers. A newly observed
   age difference alone is not a new scientific contribution.
   https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/793129
4. **Healthcare Access, Satisfaction, and Affordability: Experiences and Barriers
   of People with Aphasia**: MEPS 2019–2022 comparison of stroke survivors with
   and without aphasia; adjacent disability/access literature.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12530145/

Defensible proposed contribution: updated 2019–2025 stroke-specific estimates
of multiple healthcare affordability domains, with carefully harmonized
measurement and prespecified subgroup analyses. Whether this is sufficiently
distinct for a particular journal remains a literature/editorial judgment.
No guarantee of acceptance or 'first study' claim is made.

CDC methodological sources were read locally in full relevant sections:

- [2025 release page](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2025-nhis.html)
  confirms availability of the public-use files and the August 27, 2026 update.
- [2020 release page](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2020-nhis.html)
  distinguishes annual, partial, and longitudinal files.
- [2020 Survey Description](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2020/srvydesc-508.pdf),
  pp. 50–52: pooled cross-sectional analysis excluding followback interviews uses
  adultpart20 and WTSA_P. The ordinary 2020 annual estimates use WTFA_A.
- [2025 Survey Description](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2025/srvydesc-508.pdf),
  pp. 35 and 46: public-use PSTRAT/PPSU require no new arbitrary year suffix
  for pooling with years since 2019, despite the 2025 sample redesign.

## Manuscript literature update

The focused search was extended during manuscript preparation to the older
stroke-specific NHIS studies, the 2023 financial-stress study, and the 2025
multiple-chronic-condition NHIS analysis. Bibliographic details were checked
against PubMed, publisher pages, PMC records, NCHS documentation and the original
methods publications. This remains a focused search, not a systematic review.

The complete 13-reference working bibliography is in
`manuscript/references.json` and the editable manuscript. It includes Levine et
al. (2007 and 2013), Springer et al. (2023), Amill-Rosario et al. (2025), the 2021
stroke-prevention guideline, NCHS design/income documentation, survey and
modified-Poisson methods, predictive margins, NCHS precision guidance and STROBE.
The Springer article is in Journal of Health Care for the Poor and Underserved,
2023;34(2):625–639, doi:10.1353/hpu.2023.0054. No claim that the age disparity or
the existence of affordability barriers is novel appears in the manuscript.
